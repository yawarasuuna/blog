Data Availability Sampling is one of the crucial upgrades for Ethereum's Surge Phase of the scaling roadmap, particularly for rollups, since we need to ensure transaction data is actually available without requiring every node to download everything. 

SubnetDAS, nicknamed as PeerDAS v0 (by Francesco?), emerges as an iterative and intermediate solution during this journey. While EIP-4844 made significant strides in improving Layer 2 economics by introducing a separate fee market for blobs and limiting data retention to around 20 days, we still need to address a fundamental bottleneck: all nodes are still required to download all data. SubnetDAS aims to bridge the gap between EIP-4844 and full Danksharding by introducing a more efficient data availability sampling approach.

At its core, SubnetDAS employs a one-dimensional data layout with horizontal extension, diverging from the two-dimensional approach proposed for full Danksharding. The system organizes data into columns across subnets, fundamentally changing how data propagates through the network. Instead of broadcasting blobs as global topics, each data sample is assigned to a specific subnet, with nodes only participating in their designated subnets.

The architecture combines the distribution and sampling phases into a single operation. Under the current specification, nodes perform what's called "Custody" by selecting four columns pseudo randomly based on their node ID. This process creates accountability - peers can verify whether nodes are participating in their assigned subnets, and if they aren't, they can be downscored and disconnected from the rest, leading to a stable network backbone and guaranteeing enough nodes on all subnets. Nodes also perform additional random subnet sampling, and the current design allows flexibility for future protocol iterations while maintaining current security guarantees.

This approach achieves remarkable efficiency gains, offering approximately 8x scaling factor on the best case scenario compared to EIP-4844. Seen that nodes, with attached validators between 0 and 8, only need to download about 1/8 of the original data, it significantly reduces bandwidth strains on the CL network propagation. This efficiency doesn't come at the cost of data integrity, the system maintains strong security guarantees through its reconstruction phase, which relies on a 1/n honest assumption. Even in a standard home connection, once 50% of data becomes available, honest nodes can download, reconstruct, and reseed the complete data.

However scaling gains should be tempered, as we aim to have a security floor. Each validator will be required to custody a minimum of 8 columns and their associated subnets, or 1 column per validator, whichever is higher. This means that as the number of validators increases, each validator will need to contribute more to the network's security and consensus. Eventually, you will participate in all samples and play a role in the reconstruction phase.

While we are on the subject of security properties, it should be noted that SubnetDAS operates on two distinct levels: individual node security and global system security. For individual nodes, the security model differs between finalized and non-finalized transactions. Non-finalized transactions rely on validator custody and traditional honest majority assumptions. The same applies for the liveness of finalized transactions, however the safety guarantees become more nuanced due to query linkability, seen that nodes reveal which samples they're requesting by joining specific subnets.

However, exploiting this vulnerability requires a malicious supermajority of validators, making attacks both difficult and economically unattractive. Besides, it can be further mitigated with accountable validator custody, where validators receive specific custody duties based on their BLS signatures, to preserve their privacy and anonimity. This addition introduces economic security similar to double finality protection, where validators failing to maintain their assigned data could face slashing penalties during social forks. 

The global system security remains robust through validator custody and fast reconstruction mechanisms. Rollup safety is similarly ensured through full node sampling and custody, as data availability directly impacts rollup security properties. Even if a few nodes are tricked by unavailable data, the individual and global sampling properties ensure the overall safety of these properties.

SubnetDAS is an exciting and pivotal step towards PeerDAS and full Darnksharding. It addresses the need for DAS, reduces bandwidth strain, and ensures strong security guarantees. As an iterative solution, it demonstrates the power of incremental progress, offering immediate scaling benefits without sacrificing Ethereum's foundational security.

What makes SubnetDAS particularly compelling is its flexibility and adaptability. It builds upon  existing technologies and integrates innovations available, while providing a strong framework that can evolve as better solutions emerge. This approach ensures that Ethereum's scaling infrastructure remains dynamic, able to incorporate future advancements seamlessly.

SubnetDAS may not be the final answer, but it is another important building block in Ethereum’s roadmap, laying the groundwork for even more efficient and secure solutions. With its forward-looking design, SubnetDAS sets the stage for ongoing improvements, ensuring Ethereum's scalability and security will continue to evolve as the ecosystem grows.
